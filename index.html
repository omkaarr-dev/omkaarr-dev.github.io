SipSure

A low-cost water tester, with team Rano for the Xylem Water Hackathon

Colorimeters from Hach, Hanna and Thermo cost lab money. We're running the same reagent chemistry on much cheaper parts: the reagent turns the sample a colour, we shine an LED through it and measure how much light makes it out. Four tests for now: chromium (VI), nitrite, free chlorine and lead. Fluoride is out of version one because we didn't have the reagent.

I'm on the device hardware and firmware. The main ESP32-S3 reads an OPT101 through a 16-bit ADS1115 and runs the OLED and four buttons. A second ESP32-S3 with a camera photographs the same cuvette against a white backlight, so every number comes with a picture to check it against. The LED runs off a constant-current driver so the light is the same on every reading.

Chromium is calibrated: ten standards from 0 to 10 ppm, and absorbance came out in a straight line with R² = 0.998. Loading that curve into the device is next, then the other three tests.

ARGO

Parcel pickup at Gate 1, checked by face. Team Helios, six of us; I was project manager

ARGO logs every parcel that comes in at Plaksha's Gate 1. Gemini 2.5 Flash reads the label so the guard doesn't have to type it, and if it can't, the guard enters it by hand. The student collects it with a face scan. We surveyed students first, and most preferred a face scan to a QR code.

We built the app with Google Antigravity, an AI coding agent. My part was the system design and the user flows, then testing and debugging what it produced. Flutter, Firebase, MobileFaceNet and InsightFace underneath.

Team Kalki

Electronics for Plaksha's University Rover Challenge team, about 50 students

Co-headed the electronics subsystem from my first year until November 2026. We cleared the Preliminary Design Review and reached the System Acceptance Review with working prototypes.

My part was the drive firmware on Teensy 4.0 and Teensy 4.1: sending and receiving CAN messages with FlexCAN_T4 and reading the wheel encoders. Every board shares one pair of wires, and when two talk at once the higher-priority ID wins without corrupting anything. I also ran some basic servo PWM tests on an STM32G474RE.

Navigation robot

IM Project Competition: a navigation board and a speed race

My first version kept drifting to one side because the two motors didn't have the same top speed. So I rebuilt it on a Raspberry Pi Pico with wheel encoders, which let me match the speeds in closed loop. Three HC-SR04 sensors across the front and two IR sensors facing down.
